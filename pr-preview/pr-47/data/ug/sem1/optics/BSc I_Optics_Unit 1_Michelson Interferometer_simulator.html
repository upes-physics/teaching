2. Background, Concept & Theory

The Michelson Interferometer, invented by Albert A. Michelson, is a fundamental optical instrument based on the principle of amplitude splitting. A single coherent beam of light from a monochromatic source strikes a partially silvered beam splitter \(G_1\), dividing into two perpendicular paths of equal intensity.

One beam reflects toward a fixed mirror \(M_1\), while the second beam travels toward a movable mirror \(M_2\). Upon reflecting off their respective mirrors, both beams recombine at the beam splitter and interfere before reaching the detector or observer screen. Because the two beams originate from the same source, they remain coherent and form concentric circular interference patterns known as Haidinger fringes (fringes of equal inclination).

3. Governing Formulas & Equations

Optical Path Difference (\(\Delta\))

$$\Delta = 2d \cos\theta$$
Where \(d\) is mirror separation and \(\theta\) is inclination angle

Constructive Condition (Bright)

$$2d \cos\theta = m\lambda$$
\(m\) represents the integer order of the fringe

Destructive Condition (Dark)

$$2d \cos\theta = \left(m + \frac{1}{2}\right)\lambda$$
\(\lambda\) is the light wavelength
