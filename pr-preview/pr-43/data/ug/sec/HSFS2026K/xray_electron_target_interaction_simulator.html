1. Physics of Electron-Target Interaction

When high-velocity electrons hit the metallic anode target inside a Coolidge tube, they interact with the target atoms at a microscopic level:

Nuclear Deceleration (Bremsstrahlung)

An incident electron passes close to a heavy nucleus (atomic charge +Ze). The positive nucleus bends the electron's trajectory, causing dramatic loss of kinetic energy emitted as continuous electromagnetic photons.

Atomic Ionization & Transitions

If the incident electron energy (e · V) is greater than the $K$-shell binding energy, it knocks out an inner electron. An outer $L$-shell or $M$-shell electron drops down to fill the vacancy, emitting a discrete photon ($K_lpha$ or $K_eta$).
