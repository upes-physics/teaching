Aim

Demonstrate the effect of isotopic (atomic) mass on the superconducting critical temperature Tc, and connect it to phonon-mediated Cooper pairing.

Learning Objectives

  • State the isotope effect relation Tc ∝ M−α
  • Explain why the isotope effect implicates phonons in Cooper pairing
  • Extract the isotope exponent α from a log–log plot
  • Compare conventional (α≈0.5), anomalous, and near-zero isotope effects
  • Relate the oxygen isotope effect in cuprates to unconventional pairing

Theory

Introduction
In 1950, Maxwell and (independently) Reynolds, Serin, Wright & Nesbitt found that the critical temperature of mercury depends measurably on which isotope of Hg was used — even though all isotopes of an element have identical electronic structure. Since only the ionic mass changes between isotopes, this proved that the lattice (phonons), not electrons alone, must be involved in the pairing mechanism — direct experimental motivation for the BCS theory of phonon-mediated Cooper pairing.
The Isotope Effect Relation
Empirical power law:
Tc ∝ M−α   ⇒   Tc(M) = Tc(Mref)·(Mref/M)α
BCS theory predicts α=0.5 exactly for weak-coupling phonon-mediated superconductors, since the phonon (Debye) frequency itself scales as:
ωD ∝ √(K/M)   ⇒   θD(M) = θD(Mref)·√(Mref/M)
and Tc ∝ θD·exp[−1/N(0)V] in the BCS formula, with N(0)V essentially mass-independent.
Extracting α Experimentally
Taking logarithms of the power law gives a straight line:
ln Tc = ln[Tc(Mref)] − α·ln(M/Mref)
Plotting ln Tc against ln M for several isotopes of the same element and measuring the slope gives −α directly — the standard experimental method.
When α Deviates from 0.5
Many transition metals (Ru, Zr, Mo) show a strongly reduced isotope effect (α≈0–0.3), attributed to Coulomb pseudopotential effects and band-structure complications. High-Tc cuprates (via the oxygen isotope effect, ¹&sup6;O→¹&sup8;O substitution) show an anomalously small α near optimal doping — important evidence that conventional phonon pairing alone cannot explain their high Tc, motivating unconventional (e.g. d-wave, spin-fluctuation-mediated) pairing theories.
Common Mistakes
❌ Assuming a zero isotope effect means phonons play no role at all — it more precisely means the simple BCS phonon picture is incomplete or modified by other interactions.
❌ Confusing isotopic mass substitution with chemical doping — isotopes preserve identical electronic structure, isolating the lattice-mass dependence.
❌ Forgetting the negative sign — heavier isotopes give lower Tc, not higher.

Common Mistakes

Reporting α without specifying the reference isotope used — since the power law is exact only in ratio form, always quote Tc and M relative to a stated reference isotope.

Why Should I Care?

The isotope effect was the single piece of evidence that told Bardeen, Cooper, and Schrieffer where to look — its near-absence in cuprate superconductors is still one of the strongest clues that high-Tc superconductivity needs a fundamentally different explanation.
