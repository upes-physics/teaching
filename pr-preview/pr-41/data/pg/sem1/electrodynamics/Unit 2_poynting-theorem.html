Layer 1 — Conceptual Understanding

Poynting's Theorem & Energy Conservation

How electromagnetic fields store, transport, and exchange energy — derived from Maxwell's equations, and read as a statement of energy conservation for the electromagnetic field.

Motivation

A charging capacitor, a radiating antenna, and a beam of sunlight all move energy from one place to another without any matter necessarily changing hands. Maxwell's equations, taken together, hide a conservation law inside them — one that tells us where electromagnetic energy lives and how fast it moves. That law is Poynting's theorem, published by John Henry Poynting in 1884, and it underlies almost every technology that transmits power or information through fields rather than wires: antennas, waveguides, optical fibers, and free-space wireless links.

Physical interpretation

Reading the integral form left to right: the rate at which energy flows into a volume through its bounding surface equals the rate the fields inside store new energy, plus the rate the fields do work on free charges (Joule heating / mechanical work). Nothing is created or destroyed — energy that appears as heat or kinetic energy in the volume must have flowed in through the boundary, carried by the field itself.

The Poynting vector

S = E × H is the energy flux density, in W/m². Its direction — always perpendicular to both E and H — is the direction electromagnetic energy is instantaneously flowing. Integrating S over a surface gives the power crossing that surface.

Common misconceptions

"Current-carrying wires transport energy through the copper." In fact the metal mostly guides the fields; the energy itself flows through the surrounding space (or dielectric) as predicted by S — see the coaxial cable simulator.
"The Poynting vector is only meaningful for radiation." S is defined for any field configuration, including static ones — a charged capacitor sitting next to a magnet has a nonzero, if odd, energy circulation.
"Energy density and Poynting flux are the same quantity." u (J/m³) is how much energy sits in a region; S (W/m²) is how fast energy crosses a surface — related, but dimensionally and physically distinct.

Summary

Poynting's theorem is Maxwell's equations rewritten as an energy-accounting statement: field energy in a region changes only because energy flows across the boundary (via S) or is converted to/from other forms via J·E. Every visualization and simulator in this module is one way of watching that accounting happen in real time.

Observation

  • S is always perpendicular to the plane containing E and H, following the right-hand rule.
  • When E and H oscillate in phase (as in a propagating plane wave), S never reverses sign — energy flows steadily forward.

Interpretation

  • |S| ≈ E·H = E²/η, where η = √(μ/ε) is the medium's wave impedance (≈377 Ω in vacuum).
