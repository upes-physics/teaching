Layer 1 — Conceptual Understanding

Why the Potentials Are Not Unique

Everything in this module follows from one uncomfortable-sounding fact: the scalar potential and vector potential that you write down for a given electric and magnetic field are not the only ones that work. Infinitely many pairs reproduce the exact same, physically measurable and . Choosing a specific pair is called fixing the gauge. This page builds the idea from the ground up; the mathematics is kept light here — full derivations live in the equation panels and the visualization pages.

Learning Outcomes

  • State why A and V are under-determined by Maxwell's equations.
  • Define a gauge transformation and show it leaves E, B invariant.
  • Derive the Lorentz gauge condition and the resulting wave equations for A and V.
  • Derive the Coulomb gauge condition and the resulting Poisson equation for V.
  • Choose the correct gauge for a radiation problem versus a static-field problem, and justify the choice physically and computationally.

Motivation

Maxwell's equations are written in terms of E and B — the quantities a lab instrument actually measures. Potentials are a bookkeeping device introduced because and automatically satisfy two of the four Maxwell equations (Gauss's law for B, and Faraday's law). That convenience has a price: the map from to is many-to-one, so before you can compute anything with potentials you must decide which potential, out of an infinite family, you will use. That decision is the gauge choice.

Historical Background

Ludvig Lorenz (not Hendrik Lorentz — a famous case of a name misattached to a result) proposed the condition that now bears the "Lorentz" spelling in 1867, showing that with the right gauge choice the potentials themselves obey a wave equation with source terms travelling at speed — the mathematical seed of the idea that light is an electromagnetic wave. The Coulomb gauge (also called the radiation or transverse gauge) is older in spirit, tracing to the instantaneous-action-at-a-distance language of electrostatics, and became indispensable in the 20th century for non-relativistic quantum electrodynamics and for magnetostatic engineering design.

Gauge Freedom

Physical Interpretation

Think of A and V as a chosen "coordinate system" for describing the electromagnetic field, in the same sense that and polar both describe the same point in a plane. The point (the physical field) does not change when you change coordinates. Gauge freedom is the electromagnetic analogue: you may relabel the potentials without relabelling the field.

Fixing that freedom — choosing a gauge — is a mathematical convenience, and the two most useful choices for this course are the Lorentz gauge and the Coulomb gauge.

The Lorentz condition ties the "compressibility" of A (its divergence) to the time-rate-of-change of V, in exactly the ratio needed so that disturbances in the potentials themselves propagate causally at speed , sourced by charge and current exactly like E and B do. This is why the Lorentz gauge is the natural home for radiation and wave problems: the potentials inherit the same retarded, propagating character as the fields.

Important Observations

  • The Lorentz condition is Lorentz-covariant (frame-independent) — it treats space and time on an equal footing, matching the structure of special relativity.

  • Ideal for antennas, wave propagation, scattering, and any relativistic or manifestly covariant formulation (QED included).

Summary

The Lorentz gauge is the natural choice whenever time-varying sources radiate energy away as waves: it produces two decoupled, causally-propagating wave equations with the same mathematical form as the wave equation obeyed by E and B themselves.

The Coulomb Gauge

Learning Outcome

Derive and show V reduces to the ordinary electrostatic potential, instantaneously determined by the charge density.

Motivation

For magnetostatics, electrostatics, and many quantum-mechanical or numerical problems, the "retardation" built into the Lorentz gauge is unnecessary machinery. The Coulomb gauge trades relativistic elegance for a scalar potential that looks exactly like the electrostatic potential you already know.

Intuitive Explanation

V in the Coulomb gauge is computed as if the charge distribution at this very instant were static — no retardation, no propagation delay. This looks like it violates relativity, but it does not: V alone is not measurable, and when V and A are recombined into E, the unphysical instantaneous pieces cancel between the two potentials, leaving a fully causal, retarded E. The "cost" of a simple V is a more complicated A.

Important Observations

  • The name "Coulomb gauge" reflects that V has exactly the Coulomb's-law form, instant in time.

  • Preferred in non-relativistic quantum mechanics (Schrödinger/Pauli equation with EM fields), magnetostatics, and many finite-element / finite-difference codes for static and low-frequency problems.

Summary

The Coulomb gauge decouples the scalar potential into an ordinary, instantaneous electrostatic problem and pushes all of the radiative, retarded physics into the vector potential's transverse current source. It is the natural choice for static fields, magnetostatics, and non-relativistic quantum treatments.

Interpretation Panel

This is exactly what a link-budget or GPS timing calculation is built on: nothing at the satellite can respond to the antenna before has elapsed. The Lorentz gauge is what lets engineers compute V and A with that delay built in automatically, using the same retarded-potential formula for both — which is why radar, GPS, satellite links, and antenna design all standardise on this gauge rather than Coulomb's instantaneous one.

Observation Panel

Capacitor: uniform E between the plates and a linear V gradient, the textbook basis for dielectric and breakdown-voltage sizing. Interpretation Panel

None of these three problems ever radiates energy away, so there is no retardation to model — the simple, instantaneous Coulomb-gauge V is exactly right, and A stays purely transverse (). That is precisely why capacitor design, electrostatic field mapping, and magnet/solenoid engineering (including MRI magnets and transformers) are worked in the Coulomb gauge rather than the Lorentz gauge: the Lorentz machinery for handling propagation delay would be solving for a delay that physically does not exist here.
