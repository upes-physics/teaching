Concept: Gibbs Paradox and Indistinguishability

The Gibbs paradox arises because classical statistical mechanics can incorrectly predict an entropy increase when two samples of the same gas are allowed to mix. For two different gases, mixing creates additional accessible configurations and gives a finite entropy of mixing, $\Delta S = 2 N k_B \ln 2$ for equal amounts. For identical gases, however, exchanging identical particles does not create a physically distinguishable new microstate; incorporating the $1/N!$ correction resolves the paradox and gives $\Delta S = 0$. Thus, the paradox highlights the fundamental role of particle indistinguishability in statistical mechanics.

Mathematical Formulas Used

1. Classical Uncorrected Entropy of Mixing (Distinguishable)

$$\Delta S_{\text{classical}} = -k_B \sum_{i} N_i \ln x_i = 2 N k_B \ln 2$$

Predicts non-zero entropy increase when mixing identical gases (The Paradox).

2. Quantum Corrected Entropy of Mixing (Indistinguishable)

$$\Delta S_{\text{identical}} = 0 \quad (\text{Identical Species})$$

$$\Delta S_{\text{different}} = 2 N k_B \ln 2 \quad (\text{Different Species})$$

3. Sackur-Tetrode Equation (Absolute Entropy)

$$S = N k_B \left[ \ln\left( \frac{V}{N} \left(\frac{4\pi m U}{3 h^2 N}\right)^{3/2} \right) + \frac{5}{2} \right]$$

Derived via microstate division by $N!$. The specific volume term $\frac{V}{N}$ makes entropy strictly extensive.

Theoretical Background

The Paradox: Classical statistical mechanics treats identical particles as distinguishable trajectories in phase space. Mixing two equal volumes $V$ of the same gas leads to an unphysical entropy increase $\Delta S = 2Nk_B \ln 2$.

Quantum Resolution: Identical particles are fundamental quantum entities with overlapping wavefunctions. Dividing phase space volume $\Omega$ by $N!$ accounts for indistinguishability, ensuring $\Delta S = 0$ for identical gases.
