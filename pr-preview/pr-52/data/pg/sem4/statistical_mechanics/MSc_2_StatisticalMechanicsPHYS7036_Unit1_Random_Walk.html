Theoretical Context

A random walk is a simple model of stochastic motion in which each step is chosen randomly and independently. Although an individual trajectory is unpredictable, the collective behaviour of many walkers follows well-defined statistical laws: the mean displacement remains zero, while the mean-square displacement grows linearly with the number of steps, ($\langle x^2\rangle=N\sigma^2$). For a large number of steps, the Central Limit Theorem leads to a Gaussian position distribution, providing the microscopic basis for diffusion and the relation ($\langle x^2\rangle=2Dt$).
