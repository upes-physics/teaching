Working principle

1. RestoreThe spring pulls the mass toward equilibrium with force −kx.
2. ResistDamping opposes velocity with force −bẋ and removes energy.
3. DriveThe periodic force F₀ cos(ωt) continually supplies energy.
4. RespondAfter transients fade, the mass follows the driver with a steady amplitude and phase lag.
\[m\ddot{x}+b\dot{x}+kx=F_0\cos(\omega t)\]
Near \(\omega_0=\sqrt{\dfrac{k}{m}}\), efficient energy transfer produces resonance; damping limits the peak.

Lagrangian derivation of the equation of motion

1. Choose the generalized coordinate\(x(t)\) is the displacement from equilibrium and \(\dot{x}(t)\) is its velocity.
2. Write the energies
\[T=\frac{1}{2}m\dot{x}^{,2},\qquad V=\frac{1}{2}kx^2\]
3. Form the Lagrangian
\[L=T-V=\frac{1}{2}m\dot{x}^{,2}-\frac{1}{2}kx^2\]
4. Add non-conservative forces
\[Q_x=F_0\cos(\omega t)-b\dot{x}\]
Damping removes energy while the external driver supplies it.
5. Apply Euler–Lagrange
\[\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{x}}\right)-\frac{\partial L}{\partial x}=Q_x\]
\[\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{x}}=m\dot{x},\qquad \frac{\partial L}{\partial x}=-kx\]
6. Simplify
\[m\ddot{x}+kx=F_0\cos(\omega t)-b\dot{x}\]
\[\boxed{m\ddot{x}+b\dot{x}+kx=F_0\cos(\omega t)}\]
Equivalent Rayleigh formulation:
\[\mathcal{R}=\frac{1}{2}b\dot{x}^{,2}\]
\[\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{x}}\right)-\frac{\partial L}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{R}}{\partial\dot{x}}=F_0\cos(\omega t)\]

Real-life applications

Vehicle suspension

Springs support the vehicle while viscous dampers dissipate road-induced energy, reducing bounce and maintaining tyre contact.

Image source: Summit Racing

Tuned mass dampers

A secondary oscillator moves out of phase with a building, limiting wind- or earthquake-driven structural motion.

Image source: Motioneering

Playground swing

Air drag and pivot friction provide damping; correctly timed pushes act as periodic driving and build amplitude near resonance.

Solved postgraduate problems

Problem 1 — Complex susceptibility and absorbed power
For \(m=2\,\mathrm{kg}\), \(b=1\,\mathrm{N\,s\,m^{-1}}\), \(k=18\,\mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}\), \(F_0=4\,\mathrm{N}\), and \(\omega=2\,\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}\), find the steady amplitude, lag, and mean absorbed power.
\[\chi(\omega)=\frac{1}{k-m\omega^2+ib\omega}=\frac{1}{10+2i}\]
\[A=F_0\lvert\chi\rvert=\frac{4}{\sqrt{104}}=0.392\,\mathrm{m}\]
\[\delta=\tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{2}{10}\right)=11.31^{\circ}\]
The mean input power equals the mean dissipated power:
\[\langle P\rangle=\frac{1}{2}b\omega^2A^2=0.308\,\mathrm{W}\]
Problem 2 — Retarded Green function and impulse response
A system has \(m=1\,\mathrm{kg}\), \(b=2\,\mathrm{N\,s\,m^{-1}}\), and \(k=10\,\mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}\). Obtain its causal Green function and response to \(J\delta(t)\), where \(J=6\,\mathrm{N\,s}\).
\[\gamma=\frac{b}{2m}=1\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}\]
\[\omega_d=\sqrt{\omega_0^2-\gamma^2}=3\,\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}\]
The Green function satisfies
\[m\ddot{G}+b\dot{G}+kG=\delta(t)\]
and causality gives
\[G(t)=\Theta(t)\frac{e^{-t}\sin(3t)}{3}\]
Therefore,
\[x(t)=JG(t)=2\Theta(t)e^{-t}\sin(3t)\,\mathrm{m}\]
Problem 3 — Resonance shift, quality factor, and bandwidth
For \(m=0.5\,\mathrm{kg}\), \(k=200\,\mathrm{N\,m^{-1}}\), and \(b=2\,\mathrm{N\,s\,m^{-1}}\), calculate the displacement-resonance frequency, weak-damping quality factor, and half-power bandwidth.
\[\omega_0=\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}=20\,\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}},\qquad \gamma=\frac{b}{2m}=2\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}\]
\[\omega_r=\sqrt{\omega_0^2-2\gamma^2}=\sqrt{392}=19.80\,\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}\]
\[Q\simeq\frac{m\omega_0}{b}=5\]
\[\Delta\omega\simeq\frac{\omega_0}{Q}=\frac{b}{m}=4\,\mathrm{rad\,s^{-1}}\]
The shift below \(\omega_0\) is caused by damping.
