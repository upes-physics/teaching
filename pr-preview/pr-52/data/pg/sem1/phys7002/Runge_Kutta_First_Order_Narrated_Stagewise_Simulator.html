FIRST ORDER IVP
ODE

Model

The problem is written as \(y'=f(x,y)\), with initial value \(y(x_0)=y_0\).

ONE SLOPE
Euler

Tangent Step

Euler uses only the slope at the beginning of the interval.

FOUR SLOPES
RK4

Weighted Average

RK4 samples slopes at the start, two midpoints, and the end of the step.

1. Initial Value Problem

\[ \frac{dy}{dx}=f(x,y), \qquad y(x_0)=y_0 \]

The goal is to estimate \(y\) at points \(x_1,x_2,\ldots\), where \(x_{n+1}=x_n+h\).

2. Euler Method

\[ y_{n+1}=y_n+h f(x_n,y_n) \]

Euler is simple but less accurate because it assumes the slope remains constant across the full step.

3. Fourth-Order Runge-Kutta Method

\[ k_1=f(x_n,y_n) \] \[ k_2=f\left(x_n+\frac{h}{2},y_n+\frac{h k_1}{2}\right) \] \[ k_3=f\left(x_n+\frac{h}{2},y_n+\frac{h k_2}{2}\right) \] \[ k_4=f(x_n+h,y_n+h k_3) \] \[ y_{n+1}=y_n+\frac{h}{6}(k_1+2k_2+2k_3+k_4) \]
Key idea: RK4 does not simply follow one tangent. It forms a weighted average of four slopes, giving much better accuracy for smooth functions.

Layer 3 - Exploration & Applications

Applications of RK4

Motion and Cooling

RK4 is used to approximate velocity, displacement, cooling temperature, and other time-dependent quantities when the exact solution is difficult or inconvenient.

Population Growth

Logistic growth \(y'=ky(1-y/M)\) is a common first-order model. RK4 gives accurate population estimates with larger step sizes than Euler.

Electrical and Control Systems

First-order circuit and control models such as \(RC\) circuits can be solved numerically when the input varies with time.

Simulation Engines

RK methods are used in numerical simulation because they balance accuracy and computational cost.

Real-Life Exploration Activities

  • Compare Euler and RK4 for exponential growth with \(h=0.5\), then \(h=0.25\).
  • Use the logistic model and explain why the curve bends as \(y\) approaches its carrying capacity.
  • Observe whether RK4 remains stable for larger \(h\) compared with Euler.

Instructor & Student Support

Teaching Notes

Suggested Teaching Sequence

  1. Start with the meaning of an IVP.
  2. Show Euler as a single tangent step.
  3. Introduce RK4 as a weighted four-slope correction.
  4. Use the simulator to vary \(h\).
  5. Discuss error and convergence.

Common Errors

  • Using \(k_i\) as increments instead of slopes.
  • Forgetting the weights \(1,2,2,1\).
  • Using \(h\) twice inside the final update.
  • Comparing methods using different step sizes.
