Dynamic equilibrium

Majority carriers diffuse across the new junction, leaving fixed ions. The resulting electric field drives an opposing drift current, so the net current is zero.

Diode equation

I = IS[eVJ/(nVT) − 1]

At equilibrium, forward diffusion and reverse drift currents cancel.

  • The depletion region contains fixed ions but very few mobile carriers.
  • The built-in field points from the positive donor ions on the N side toward the negative acceptor ions on the P side.

Modeling note: numerical material constants, saturation current and breakdown voltage are educational approximations. Real devices depend on geometry, fabrication, area and datasheet limits.

PN junction concepts

A PN junction is the boundary formed when P-type and N-type semiconductors are joined. Its behaviour follows from diffusion, drift and the electric field of uncovered ions.

Core theory
1

Junction formation

Holes diffuse from P to N and electrons diffuse from N to P. Recombination near the boundary removes mobile carriers and exposes immobile acceptor and donor ions.

↔

Depletion region

The region around the junction is depleted of most mobile carriers. Its fixed negative ions on the P side and fixed positive ions on the N side create the built-in field.

W ∝ √[(Vbi − VJ)(1/NA + 1/ND)]

0

Equilibrium

Diffusion tends to move majority carriers across the junction, while the built-in electric field drives a drift current in the opposite direction. The two currents balance.

＋

Forward bias

Connect P to the positive terminal and N to the negative terminal. The applied field opposes the built-in field, lowers the barrier and narrows the depletion region. Majority-carrier current rises exponentially.

−

Reverse bias

Connect P to the negative terminal and N to the positive terminal. The barrier grows and the depletion region widens. Majority carriers are blocked; only a small minority-carrier current flows before breakdown.

⚡

Breakdown

Avalanche breakdown comes from impact ionisation in a strong field. In a heavily doped, very narrow junction, quantum tunnelling can dominate and is called Zener breakdown. External resistance must limit current.

eˣ

Diode I–V law

The idealised current is exponential in forward bias and approximately −IS in ordinary reverse bias. The thermal voltage VT = kT/q increases with temperature.

I = IS(eV/(nVT) − 1)

T

Temperature effect

Higher temperature increases intrinsic carriers and reverse saturation current. For the same forward current, a real silicon diode generally requires a lower forward voltage as temperature rises.

N/P

Unequal doping

Charge neutrality requires equal uncovered charge on both sides. Therefore, the depletion region spreads farther into the more lightly doped material.

NAxp = NDxn

What is a majority carrier?

Holes are the majority carriers in P-type material, while electrons are the majority carriers in N-type material. Minority carriers are the much less numerous opposite carrier type.

Why are the ions called fixed?

The ionised dopant atoms are part of the crystal lattice. They contribute charge but do not move through the material like electrons and holes.

Why is a series resistor important?

The diode current can rise extremely quickly in forward conduction or breakdown. A resistor absorbs part of the source voltage and prevents destructive current.

Five-question knowledge check

Choose one answer for each question, then submit to view your score and explanations.

5 questions
1. What is found inside the depletion region of an ideal PN junction?

Recombination removes many mobile carriers near the boundary, leaving fixed negative acceptor ions on the P side and fixed positive donor ions on the N side.

2. Which change occurs when a PN junction is forward biased?

Forward bias opposes the built-in electric field. This lowers the energy barrier and lets majority carriers cross the junction more easily.

3. Before breakdown, reverse current is mainly due to:

Reverse bias blocks majority carriers. A small current still flows because minority carriers are generated thermally and swept across by the electric field.

4. If the P side is much more heavily doped than the N side, where does most of the depletion region lie?

The uncovered charge must balance: NAxp = NDxn. The lighter-doped side therefore needs a larger width.

5. Which statement best distinguishes avalanche and Zener breakdown?

Avalanche multiplication results from energetic carriers colliding with the lattice. Zener breakdown is quantum tunnelling through a very narrow depletion region.
