Photoelectric effect as a radiation interaction process

Model of how photons (X-rays / gamma rays) interact with shielding material via the photoelectric effect: a photon transfers all its energy to a bound inner-shell electron, ejecting a photoelectron (KE = hf − Ebinding) and leaving a vacancy that is filled by emission of a characteristic X-ray or Auger electron — a secondary radiation source relevant to shielding design.

Why this matters for shielding: photoelectric absorption probability scales roughly with Z4–Z5 and falls off steeply as 1/E3. That is why dense, high-Z materials like lead are effective shielding at diagnostic and low gamma energies, and why the same shield becomes far less effective as photon energy rises (Compton scattering starts to dominate instead, which this simulator does not model). It's also why photoelectric absorption generates secondary characteristic X-rays inside the shield — a real consideration in shield and detector design, since that secondary radiation still needs to be attenuated or accounted for.
Interaction probabilities and binding energies here are simplified/illustrative (scaled from a Z4/E3 trend), not exact cross-section data — use NIST XCOM or an attenuation coefficient table for real dose/shielding calculations. h = 4.1357 × 10-15 eV·s.
