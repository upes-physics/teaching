2. Background, Concept & Theory

Fraunhofer Diffraction occurs when both the light source and the viewing screen are effectively at infinite distances from the diffracting aperture (or when lenses are placed to produce parallel incident and diffracted beams).

Key Physical Concepts:

  • Single Slit Diffraction Factor: Secondary wavelets originating across a single slit of width \(a\) interfere to form a single-slit diffraction pattern modulated by \(\alpha = \frac{\pi a \sin\theta}{\lambda}\):
    \(I_{\text{single}}(\theta) = \left( \frac{\sin\alpha}{\alpha} \right)^2\)
  • Multiple Slit Interference Factor: For \(N\) parallel slits spaced a distance \(d\) center-to-center, phase differences between adjacent slits introduce an interference factor governed by \(\beta = \frac{\pi d \sin\theta}{\lambda}\):
    \(I_{\text{multi}}(\theta) = \left( \frac{\sin(N\beta)}{\sin\beta} \right)^2\)
  • Combined Total Intensity: The total observed field pattern is the product of the single-slit diffraction envelope and the multi-slit interference factor:
    \(I(\theta) = I_0 \cdot \left( \frac{\sin\alpha}{\alpha} \right)^2 \cdot \left( \frac{\sin(N\beta)}{N\sin\beta} \right)^2\)
  • Missing Orders (Absent Spectra): When a principal interference maximum (\(d\sin\theta = m\lambda\)) coincides with a single-slit diffraction minimum (\(a\sin\theta = n\lambda\)), that order becomes invisible because the diffraction envelope goes to zero. The condition for missing orders is:
    \(\frac{d}{a} = \frac{m}{n}\)  \(\implies m = n \cdot \left(\frac{d}{a}\right)\)
3. Governing Formulas & Equations

Fraunhofer Intensity Equation

$$I(\theta) = I_0 \left(\frac{\sin\alpha}{\alpha}\right)^2 \left(\frac{\sin N\beta}{N\sin\beta}\right)^2$$
\(\alpha = \frac{\pi a \sin\theta}{\lambda}\), \(\beta = \frac{\pi d \sin\theta}{\lambda}\)

Principal Maxima Condition

$$d \sin\theta = m \lambda \quad (m = 0, \pm 1, \pm 2, \dots)$$
Bright sharp interference peaks for $N$ slits

Diffraction Minima Envelope

$$a \sin\theta = n \lambda \quad (n = \pm 1, \pm 2, \dots)$$
Enclosing single-slit envelope zeros

Missing Orders Condition

$$m = n \left(\frac{d}{a}\right)$$
Order $m$ disappears when ratio $\frac{d}{a}$ is an integer
