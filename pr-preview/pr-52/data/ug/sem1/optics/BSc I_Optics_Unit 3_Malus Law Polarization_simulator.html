2. Background, Concept & Theory

Malus's Law states that when a completely plane-polarized light wave is incident on an analyzer, the intensity \(I\) of light transmitted through the analyzer varies directly as the square of the cosine of the angle \(\theta\) between the transmission axis of the polarizer and the transmission axis of the analyzer.

Key Physical Concepts:

  • Unpolarized Light to Polarized Light: Natural light from an incandescent or sodium source contains electric field vectors oscillating randomly in all transverse planes. Passing unpolarized light through an initial ideal polarizer reduces its intensity by half (\(I_0 = \frac{1}{2} I_{\text{unpolarized}}\)) and aligns all electric field vectors along a single axis.
  • Electric Field Vector Resolution: When linearly polarized light with electric field amplitude \(E_0\) encounters an analyzer set at an angle \(\theta\) relative to the polarizer axis, the field vector breaks into two perpendicular components:
    • Parallel component: \(E = E_0 \cos\theta\) (transmitted by analyzer).
    • Perpendicular component: \(E_\perp = E_0 \sin\theta\) (absorbed or reflected by analyzer).
  • Intensity Relationship: Since light wave intensity is proportional to the square of its electric field amplitude (\(I \propto E^2\)), the transmitted intensity is given by:
    \(I = I_0 \cos^2\theta\)
  • Crossed Polarizers (\(\theta = 90^\circ, 270^\circ\)): When the analyzer is perpendicular to the polarizer, \(\cos 90^\circ = 0\), leading to total light extinction (\(I = 0\)).
3. Governing Formulas & Equations

Malus's Law Equation

$$I(\theta) = I_0 \cos^2\theta$$
\(I_0\): Incident polarized intensity, \(\theta\): Relative axis angle

Transmitted Electric Field Amplitude

$$E(\theta) = E_0 \cos\theta$$
\(E_0\): Incident electric field amplitude

Transmission Ratio & Percentage

$$\frac{I}{I_0} = \cos^2\theta \implies \text{Transmission (\%)} = 100 \cdot \cos^2\theta$$
Independent of total source power
