2. Background, Concept & Theory

Young's Double Slit Experiment demonstrates the wave nature of light through division of wavefront. Monochromatic light passing through a pinhole falls upon two closely spaced parallel slits \(S_1\) and \(S_2\), acting as two mutually coherent light sources.

Light waves emerging from these slits overlap in the region beyond the slit plane. Where wave crests meet crests (in-phase superposition), constructive interference occurs, yielding bright fringes. Where crests meet troughs (out-of-phase superposition), destructive interference occurs, yielding dark regions.

3. Governing Formulas & Equations

Intensity Distribution (\(I\))

$$I = I_0 \cos^2\left(\frac{\pi d y}{\lambda D}\right)$$
Where \(y\) is position on screen & \(I_0\) is maximum intensity

Fringe Width (\(\beta\))

$$\beta = \frac{\lambda D}{d}$$
Separation between consecutive bright or dark fringes

Path Difference (\(\Delta\))

$$\Delta = \frac{d \cdot y}{D}$$
Difference in distance traveled by light from \(S_1\) and \(S_2\)
