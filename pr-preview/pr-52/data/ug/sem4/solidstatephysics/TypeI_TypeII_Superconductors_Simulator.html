Theory

Introduction — Meissner Effect
Below Tc and below a critical field, a superconductor expels all magnetic flux from its interior (B=0 inside) via persistent surface screening currents — the Meissner effect. This makes it a perfect diamagnet (χ=−1), not just a perfect conductor: a perfect conductor would merely trap whatever flux was present when it cooled through Tc, but a superconductor actively expels pre-existing flux too.
Type-I: Single Critical Field
Complete Meissner state up to a single thermodynamic critical field Hc; above it, superconductivity is destroyed abruptly (first-order transition):
−M = H  (H<Hc)   |   M = 0  (H>Hc)
Temperature dependence (empirical two-fluid law):
Hc(T) = Hc(0)[1−(T/Tc)²]
Occurs when the Ginzburg–Landau parameter κ = λ/ξ < 1/√2 (positive domain-wall surface energy disfavours flux penetration).
Type-II: Vortex (Mixed) State
For κ > 1/√2, flux penetrates as quantized vortices above a lower critical field Hc1, each carrying one flux quantum:
Φ0 = h/2e = 2.068×10⁻¹⁵ Wb
Vortices arrange in a triangular Abrikosov lattice with spacing av = √(2Φ0/√3B). Superconductivity survives (in filaments around each vortex core) up to the upper critical field:
Hc2 = √2·κ·Hc
Common Mistakes
❌ Thinking Type-II superconductivity "ends" at Hc1 — it persists (in the mixed state) all the way to the much larger Hc2.
❌ Confusing a perfect conductor (R=0 only) with a superconductor (R=0 and flux expulsion) — the Meissner effect is the extra ingredient.
❌ Assuming vortices are randomly placed — they form an ordered triangular lattice to minimise mutual repulsive energy.

Common Mistakes

Drawing the Type-II magnetization curve dropping straight to zero at Hc1 — it should decrease gradually through the mixed state, reaching zero only at the much larger Hc2.

Why Should I Care?

Every MRI magnet and the LHC's bending magnets rely on Type-II superconductors (NbTi, Nb₃Sn) precisely because their huge Hc2 lets them carry enormous currents in multi-tesla fields without quenching — a Type-I material like lead would revert to normal at a tiny fraction of that field.
