01 · Foundations

What is a JFET?

Unlike the BJT, the JFET is controlled by a voltage, not a current — and conduction relies on only one type of charge carrier, which is why it's called a unipolar device.

Construction

A bar of lightly doped semiconductor — the channel — carries current between two ohmic contacts: Source (S) and Drain (D).

  • Heavily doped regions of the opposite type are diffused into both sides of the bar, tied together internally to form a single Gate (G).
  • n-channel JFET: n-type bar, p+ gate regions.
  • p-channel JFET: p-type bar, n+ gate regions — same idea, polarities reversed.
  • Only three terminals — there is no separate base-like control current path.

Working principle

The gate–channel junction is always kept reverse biased. Reverse bias widens the depletion region into the channel from both sides, narrowing the conducting path.

  • Increasing |VGS| → wider depletion region → narrower channel → less ID.
  • Because current flows along the channel, the reverse bias is not uniform — it is largest near the drain.
  • At a large enough VDS the two depletion regions meet at the drain end: the channel is pinched off, and ID levels out.
  • No gate current flows in normal operation → extremely high input resistance.

Key quantities

Two numbers fully describe a given JFET's transfer behaviour:

  • I_DSS — drain current when VGS = 0 (channel fully open, maximum current).
  • V_P (V_GS(off)) — the pinch-off / cut-off voltage, the VGS at which ID falls to zero.
  • g_m — transconductance, ΔID/ΔVGS, the JFET's "gain".
Shockley's equationI_D = I_DSS·(1 − V_GS/V_P)²
Drain saturation voltageV_DS(sat) = V_GS − V_P
Transconductanceg_m = ΔI_D / ΔV_GS
Cut-off conditionI_D → 0 as V_GS → V_P
02 · Regions of operation

Ohmic, saturation, cut-off

A JFET's drain characteristic splits cleanly into three regions depending on VDS relative to VDS(sat), and on how close VGS is to VP.

Ohmic / triode

VDS < VDS(sat). The channel is not yet pinched off, so the JFET behaves like a voltage-controlled resistor — ID rises roughly linearly with VDS.

Saturation / active

VDS ≥ VDS(sat). The channel is pinched off at the drain end; ID becomes almost independent of VDS and follows Shockley's equation. This is the region used for amplification.

Cut-off

VGS ≤ VP. The depletion regions meet along the entire channel even at VDS = 0 — the channel is closed and ID ≈ 0 regardless of VDS.
