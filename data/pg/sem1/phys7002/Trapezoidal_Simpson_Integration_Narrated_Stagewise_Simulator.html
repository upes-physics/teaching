Layer 1 - Conceptual Understanding

Numerical Integration by Trapezoidal and Simpson Rules

Numerical integration estimates the area under a curve when exact integration is difficult or when function values are known only at tabulated points.

Learning Outcomes

  • Approximate \(\int_a^b f(x)\,dx\) using composite Trapezoidal rule.
  • Apply Simpson's 1/3 rule when the number of subintervals is even.
  • Apply Simpson's 3/8 rule when the number of subintervals is a multiple of 3.
  • Compare approximate values with exact/reference values.
  • Analyze convergence as \(n\) increases and \(h=(b-a)/n\) decreases.

CUBIC-STYLE PANEL
3/8

Simpson's 3/8

Groups of three intervals use weights \(1,3,3,1\).

1. Composite Trapezoidal Rule

\[ I \approx \frac{h}{2}\left[y_0+y_n+2(y_1+y_2+\cdots+y_{n-1})\right] \]

2. Composite Simpson's 1/3 Rule

\[ I \approx \frac{h}{3}\left[y_0+y_n+4(y_1+y_3+\cdots+y_{n-1})+2(y_2+y_4+\cdots+y_{n-2})\right] \]
Condition: \(n\) must be even.

3. Composite Simpson's 3/8 Rule

\[ I \approx \frac{3h}{8}\left[y_0+y_n+3\sum_{i\not\equiv0\pmod3}y_i+2\sum_{i\equiv0\pmod3,\ i\ne0,n}y_i\right] \]
Condition: \(n\) must be a multiple of 3.

Layer 3 - Exploration & Applications

Applications

Tabulated Data

When only measured values are available, numerical quadrature estimates area from sampled data points.

Work and Distance

Integrals such as work from force-displacement data or distance from velocity-time data are often evaluated numerically.

Signal and Energy Calculations

Engineers use numerical integration for sampled waveforms, power, energy, and response curves.

Area Under Density Curves

Probabilities for continuous random variables are areas under probability density functions.

Instructor & Student Support

Teaching Notes

Suggested Sequence

  1. Begin with the meaning of area under a curve.
  2. Show trapezoids as straight-line panels.
  3. Introduce Simpson's 1/3 as parabolic fitting over two intervals.
  4. Introduce Simpson's 3/8 over three intervals.
  5. Use convergence comparison to discuss \(h\), \(n\), and error.

Common Errors

  • Using Simpson's 1/3 rule with odd \(n\).
  • Using Simpson's 3/8 rule when \(n\) is not a multiple of 3.
  • Forgetting the endpoint weights.
  • Comparing rules with different intervals or different \(n\).
