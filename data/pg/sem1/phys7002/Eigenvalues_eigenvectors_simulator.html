Layer 1 — Conceptual Understanding

Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors

A matrix transforms space. Most directions rotate or shear into new directions, but an eigenvector keeps its line. The matrix may stretch, shrink, or reverse it, and the corresponding stretch factor is the eigenvalue.

Layer 3 — Applications

Where the Same Idea Appears

Application 1 · Stability of a Linear System

For , eigenvectors are the natural motion directions. If every eigenvalue has negative real part, trajectories decay toward equilibrium. If at least one has positive real part, disturbances grow.

Application 2 · Principal Component Analysis

A covariance matrix is symmetric, so its eigenvectors are perpendicular principal axes. Larger eigenvalues identify directions containing more variance in the data.

Application 3 · Stress, Inertia, and Principal Axes

In mechanics, symmetric matrices often represent stress or inertia tensors. Eigenvectors give the special axes where coupling terms disappear, making the physical interpretation much cleaner.
