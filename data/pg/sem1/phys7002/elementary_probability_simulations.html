Layer 1 — Conceptual Understanding

Elementary Probability Theory

Probability measures how likely an event is before an experiment occurs. Simulation lets us repeat a random experiment many times and compare observed relative frequency with the theoretical probability.

theoretical probabilityexperimental frequencyrandom variationlong-run trend

Learning Outcomes

  • Define sample space, event, probability, and complement.
  • Compare theoretical probability with experimental probability.
  • Simulate coin tosses, dice rolls, urn draws, and binomial trials.
  • Observe the law of large numbers through repeated simulation.
  • Compute expected value and variance for simple discrete distributions.

Layer 3 — Applications

Where Elementary Probability Appears

Application 1 · Quality Control

Repeated inspection is naturally binomial: an item either passes or fails. Probability models estimate defect counts and help decide sampling plans.

Application 2 · Games of Chance

Dice, cards, and coin games use sample spaces, equally likely outcomes, expected value, and long-run frequency.

Application 3 · Risk and Decision Making

Weather forecasts, insurance, reliability, and medical screening all distinguish probability before the event from observed frequency after repeated events.
