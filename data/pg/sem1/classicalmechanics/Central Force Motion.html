1. Fundamental Theory & Central Forces

Key Concepts of Central Forces

  • Definition & Spherical Symmetry: A central force is a force directed along the line joining the origin to the particle, whose magnitude depends only on the distance $r$ from the origin ($\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}) = F(r)\hat{\mathbf{r}}$). Because it depends purely on radial distance, the potential $V(r)$ is spherically symmetric.
  • Conservation of Angular Momentum ($\mathbf{L}$): Since the force is purely radial, the torque about the origin is identically zero ($\boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}) = \mathbf{0}$). This guarantees that angular momentum $L = \mu r^2 \dot{\theta}$ is conserved, restricting the particle's entire trajectory to a fixed 2D plane.
  • Effective 1D Radial Reduction: By substituting the conserved angular momentum into the kinetic energy equation, the complex 2D planar motion is reduced to an equivalent 1D radial problem governed by the effective potential: $$V_{\text{eff}}(r) = V(r) + \frac{L^2}{2\mu r^2}$$ where $\frac{L^2}{2\mu r^2}$ acts as a repulsive "centrifugal barrier" preventing the particle from collapsing into the origin.

Step-by-Step Derivation

Physical System: A particle of reduced mass $\mu$ moving in two dimensions under a central potential field $V(r) = -k/r$.

Step-by-Step Derivation of Kinetic Energy $T$ in Polar Coordinates

Step 1.1: Coordinate Transformation

We map Cartesian coordinates $(x, y)$ to plane polar coordinates $(r, \theta)$ via:

$$x = r \cos\theta, \quad y = r \sin\theta$$
Step 1.2: Differentiating Position with Respect to Time

Using the product rule and chain rule of differentiation ($\dot{x} = \frac{dx}{dt}$):

$$\dot{x} = \dot{r} \cos\theta - r \dot{\theta} \sin\theta$$ $$\dot{y} = \dot{r} \sin\theta + r \dot{\theta} \cos\theta$$
Step 1.3: Computing $v^2 = \dot{x}^2 + \dot{y}^2$

Squaring $\dot{x}$:

$$\dot{x}^2 = \dot{r}^2 \cos^2\theta - 2 r \dot{r} \dot{\theta} \sin\theta \cos\theta + r^2 \dot{\theta}^2 \sin^2\theta$$

Squaring $\dot{y}$:

$$\dot{y}^2 = \dot{r}^2 \sin^2\theta + 2 r \dot{r} \dot{\theta} \sin\theta \cos\theta + r^2 \dot{\theta}^2 \cos^2\theta$$

Adding $\dot{x}^2$ and $\dot{y}^2$ together cancels the cross-terms ($-2 r \dot{r} \dot{\theta} \sin\theta \cos\theta$ and $+2 r \dot{r} \dot{\theta} \sin\theta \cos\theta$):

$$v^2 = \dot{x}^2 + \dot{y}^2 = \dot{r}^2 (\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta) + r^2 \dot{\theta}^2 (\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta)$$

Applying the Pythagorean identity $\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta = 1$ yields:

$$v^2 = \dot{r}^2 + r^2 \dot{\theta}^2$$
Step 1.4: Final Kinetic Energy Expression

Substituting $v^2$ into the standard kinetic energy equation $T = \frac{1}{2} \mu v^2$ gives:

$$T(r, \theta, \dot{r}, \dot{\theta}) = \frac{1}{2} \mu \left( \dot{r}^2 + r^2 \dot{\theta}^2 \right)$$

Step-by-Step Euler-Lagrange Derivation for Equations of Motion

Step 2.1: Constructing the System Lagrangian

For a standard central potential $V(r) = -k/r$, the Lagrangian $\mathcal{L} = T - V$ is explicitly:

$$\mathcal{L}(r, \theta, \dot{r}, \dot{\theta}) = \frac{1}{2} \mu \dot{r}^2 + \frac{1}{2} \mu r^2 \dot{\theta}^2 + \frac{k}{r}$$
Step 2.2: Angular Equation of Motion ($\theta$-Coordinate)

The Euler-Lagrange equation for coordinate $\theta$ is:

$$\frac{d}{dt}\left( \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot{\theta}} \right) - \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \theta} = 0$$

1. Compute partial derivative with respect to $\dot{\theta}$:

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot{\theta}} = \mu r^2 \dot{\theta}$$

2. Compute partial derivative with respect to $\theta$:

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \theta} = 0 \quad (\theta \text{ is a cyclic coordinate})$$

3. Substitute into the Euler-Lagrange equation:

$$\frac{d}{dt}\left( \mu r^2 \dot{\theta} \right) = 0 \implies \mu r^2 \dot{\theta} = L \quad (\text{Constant Angular Momentum})$$

Isolating angular velocity $\dot{\theta}$:

$$\dot{\theta} = \frac{L}{\mu r^2}$$
Step 2.3: Radial Equation of Motion ($r$-Coordinate)

The Euler-Lagrange equation for coordinate $r$ is:

$$\frac{d}{dt}\left( \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot{r}} \right) - \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial r} = 0$$

1. Compute terms:

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot{r}} = \mu \dot{r} \implies \frac{d}{dt}\left( \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot{r}} \right) = \mu \ddot{r}$$ $$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial r} = \mu r \dot{\theta}^2 - \frac{k}{r^2}$$

2. Set up equation of motion:

$$\mu \ddot{r} - \left( \mu r \dot{\theta}^2 - \frac{k}{r^2} \right) = 0 \implies \mu \ddot{r} = \mu r \dot{\theta}^2 - \frac{k}{r^2}$$

3. Substitute conserved $\dot{\theta} = \frac{L}{\mu r^2}$ into the centripetal term $\mu r \dot{\theta}^2$:

$$\mu r \dot{\theta}^2 = \mu r \left(\frac{L}{\mu r^2}\right)^2 = \frac{L^2}{\mu r^3}$$

4. Resulting 1D radial force equation:

$$\mu \ddot{r} = \frac{L^2}{\mu r^3} - \frac{k}{r^2}$$
Step 2.4: Formulation of Effective Potential $V_{\text{eff}}(r)$

Recalling $F_r = \mu\ddot{r} = -\frac{d V_{\text{eff}}(r)}{dr}$, we integrate the radial forces with respect to $r$:

$$V_{\text{eff}}(r) = V(r) + \frac{L^2}{2 \mu r^2} = -\frac{k}{r} + \frac{L^2}{2 \mu r^2}$$

Where $\frac{L^2}{2 \mu r^2}$ is the centrifugal potential barrier and $V(r) = -k/r$ is the attractive central potential.

4. Core Problem Set

Comprehensive Problem: Orbit Stability & Small Radial Oscillations

A particle of reduced mass $\mu$ moves in a central power-law potential $V(r) = k r^n$, where $k$ and $n$ are constants ($k > 0$ for $n > 0$, and $k < 0$ for $n < 0$).

Tasks:

  1. Effective Potential Formulation: Construct the effective potential $V_{\text{eff}}(r)$ for this system with angular momentum $L$. Derive the condition for the equilibrium radius $r_0$ of a circular orbit in terms of $\mu, L, k,$ and $n$.
  2. Stability Criterion: By analyzing the second derivative $\left.\frac{d^2 V_{\text{eff}}}{dr^2}\right|_{r=r_0}$, prove that stable circular orbits can only exist if $n > -2$. What physical disaster occurs if $n \le -2$?
  3. Frequency of Small Oscillations: For a slightly perturbed circular orbit ($r(t) = r_0 + \eta(t)$ with $|\eta| \ll r_0$), perform a Taylor expansion of $V_{\text{eff}}(r)$ around $r_0$ to show that small radial displacements satisfy a simple harmonic oscillator equation $\ddot{\eta} + \omega_r^2 \eta = 0$. Express the radial oscillation frequency $\omega_r$ in terms of the orbital frequency $\omega_\theta = \dot{\theta} = \frac{L}{\mu r_0^2}$.
  4. Bertrand's Theorem Connection: Evaluate the ratio $\frac{\omega_r}{\omega_\theta}$ for the Kepler potential ($n = -1$) and the 3D isotropic harmonic oscillator potential ($n = 2$). Explain physically why only these two potential exponents produce completely closed bound orbits for arbitrary non-circular initial conditions.
