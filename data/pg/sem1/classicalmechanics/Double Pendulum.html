01 / Lagrangian setup — kinetic & potential energy (before subtracting)

System & coordinates

Two point masses m₁, m₂ on massless rigid rods of length L₁, L₂, hinged in series from a fixed pivot. θ₁, θ₂ are measured from the downward vertical. Bob-1 position depends on θ₁ alone; bob-2 position depends on both θ₁ and θ₂, since it hangs off the end of rod 1. This app uses m₁=m₂=m and L₁=L₂=L (concretely m=1 kg, L=1 m in the code), so the derivation below is carried symbolically first, then specialized to that equal-mass, equal-length case.
θ₁, θ₂ shown from the downward vertical at their own hinge, matching the sign convention used in every formula below (y is positive upward, so a hanging bob has negative y).
x₁ = L₁ sin θ₁   ,   y₁ = −L₁ cos θ₁
x₂ = L₁ sin θ₁ + L₂ sin θ₂   ,   y₂ = −L₁ cos θ₁ − L₂ cos θ₂
Differentiating in time gives each bob's velocity components:
ẋ₁ = L₁θ̇₁cos θ₁   ,   ẏ₁ = L₁θ̇₁sin θ₁
ẋ₂ = L₁θ̇₁cos θ₁ + L₂θ̇₂cos θ₂   ,   ẏ₂ = L₁θ̇₁sin θ₁ + L₂θ̇₂sin θ₂

Kinetic energy — T

Each bob contributes ½m(ẋ²+ẏ²). Bob 1's speed only involves θ̇₁:
T₁ = ½m₁(ẋ₁²+ẏ₁²) = ½m₁L₁²θ̇₁²
Bob 2's speed mixes θ̇₁ and θ̇₂ — squaring ẋ₂²+ẏ₂² and using cos(θ₁−θ₂) via the angle-difference identity leaves a coupling term:
T₂ = ½m₂[L₁²θ̇₁² + L₂²θ̇₂² + 2L₁L₂θ̇₁θ̇₂cos(θ₁−θ₂)]
▼ T = T₁ + T₂ ▼
T = ½(m₁+m₂)L₁²θ̇₁² + ½m₂L₂²θ̇₂² + m₂L₁L₂θ̇₁θ̇₂cos(θ₁−θ₂)

Potential energy — V

Taking the pivot as the height reference (gravity acts along −y), each bob's potential energy is m·g·y, using the y-coordinates above:
V₁ = m₁gy₁ = −m₁gL₁cos θ₁
V₂ = m₂gy₂ = −m₂g(L₁cos θ₁ + L₂cos θ₂)
▼ V = V₁ + V₂ ▼
V = −(m₁+m₂)gL₁cos θ₁ − m₂gL₂cos θ₂

Specializing to this system — m₁=m₂=m, L₁=L₂=L

Substituting equal masses and equal lengths collapses both energies to a single mass/length scale:
T
T = mL²θ̇₁² + ½mL²θ̇₂² + mL²θ̇₁θ̇₂cos(θ₁−θ₂)
T = mL²[ θ̇₁² + ½θ̇₂² + θ̇₁θ̇₂cos(θ₁−θ₂) ]
V
V = −2mgLcos θ₁ − mgLcos θ₂
V = −mgL(2cos θ₁ + cos θ₂)

The Lagrangian — L = T − V

Subtracting the general forms above:
L = ½(m₁+m₂)L₁²θ̇₁² + ½m₂L₂²θ̇₂² + m₂L₁L₂θ̇₁θ̇₂cos(θ₁−θ₂) + (m₁+m₂)gL₁cos θ₁ + m₂gL₂cos θ₂
▼ for m₁=m₂=m, L₁=L₂=L ▼
L = mL²[ θ̇₁² + ½θ̇₂² + θ̇₁θ̇₂cos(θ₁−θ₂) ] + mgL(2cos θ₁ + cos θ₂)
Running this through the Euler–Lagrange equation for each generalized coordinate, d/dt(∂L/∂θ̇ᵢ) − ∂L/∂θᵢ = 0, produces the two coupled angular-acceleration equations this app actually integrates (RK4, page 02 onward) to animate the pendulum and measure how fast two nearly-identical release angles pull apart.

Equations of motion — applying d/dt(∂L/∂θ̇ᵢ) − ∂L/∂θᵢ = 0

Applying Euler–Lagrange to θ₁ (i=1) and θ₂ (i=2) in turn — differentiating L with respect to θ̇ᵢ, taking that result's time derivative, subtracting ∂L/∂θᵢ, and then solving the resulting pair of linear equations in θ̈₁ and θ̈₂ simultaneously — yields the general two-mass, two-length angular accelerations:
θ̈₁  =  [ −g(2m₁+m₂)sin θ₁ − m₂g sin(θ₁−2θ₂) − 2 sin(θ₁−θ₂)·m₂(θ̇₂²L₂ + θ̇₁²L₁cos(θ₁−θ₂)) ]
     / [ L₁(2m₁+m₂−m₂cos(2θ₁−2θ₂)) ]
θ̈₂  =  [ 2 sin(θ₁−θ₂)·( θ̇₁²L₁(m₁+m₂) + g(m₁+m₂)cos θ₁ + θ̇₂²L₂m₂cos(θ₁−θ₂) ) ]
     / [ L₂(2m₁+m₂−m₂cos(2θ₁−2θ₂)) ]
▼ for m₁=m₂=m, L₁=L₂=L ▼
θ̈₁  =  [ −3g sin θ₁ − g sin(θ₁−2θ₂) − 2L sin(θ₁−θ₂)·(θ̇₂² + θ̇₁²cos(θ₁−θ₂)) ] / [ L(3−cos(2θ₁−2θ₂)) ]
θ̈₂  =  [ 2 sin(θ₁−θ₂)·(2Lθ̇₁² + 2g cos θ₁ + Lθ̇₂²cos(θ₁−θ₂)) ] / [ L(3−cos(2θ₁−2θ₂)) ]
These two second-order ODEs (plus θ̇₁, θ̇₂ themselves) form the 4-variable state vector [θ₁, θ̇₁, θ₂, θ̇₂] that derivs() evaluates and rk4() steps forward at dt = 0.0015 s — exactly the a1/a2 expressions in the code below, with m₁=m₂=1 kg and L₁=L₂=1 m.

04 / Lyapunov exponent — full derivation, per-angle table

Divergence model

δ(t) = δ₀ · e^(λt)
Two pendulums are released under the same constraint θ₁=θ₂. The one physical perturbation is a 1 Å arc-length nudge on θ₁' alone — θ₂' isn't nudged separately, it just stays on the same θ1=θ2 line, so it inherits the identical offset by construction. δ(t) is the Euclidean separation of bob 2, √(Δx₂²+Δy₂²) — the same metric used on pages 01–02 — tracked in meters as the two runs pull apart.
