Nuclear forces: key properties (quick revision)

Inside the nucleus, nucleons are bound mainly by the strong nuclear force, which is very strong but acts only over a short range. Protons additionally feel Coulomb repulsion, which is long-range. Alpha decay happens because an alpha cluster can sometimes escape the nucleus by quantum tunneling through the Coulomb barrier.

  • Short range (~1–2 fm): strong force falls rapidly with distance.
  • Saturation: each nucleon interacts mainly with nearby nucleons (helps explain roughly constant nuclear density).
  • Repulsive core: at very small separation, force becomes repulsive (prevents collapse).
  • Charge-independence (approx.): strong force is nearly the same for pp, nn, and np after removing Coulomb.

Nuclear Force vs Distance (Physical Picture)

The nature of nuclear force can be understood by studying how it varies with distance between two nucleons. Unlike electromagnetic forces, the nuclear force is short-ranged, strongly attractive at intermediate distances, and repulsive at very short distances.

Physical Interpretation

  • Very short distance (r < 0.5 fm): Strong repulsive core prevents nucleons from collapsing into each other.
  • Intermediate region (0.5 – 2 fm): Strong attractive nuclear force binds nucleons together and is responsible for nuclear stability.
  • Large distance (r > 2 fm): Nuclear force rapidly drops to zero → shows its short-range nature.
  • Coulomb force (red curve): Always repulsive for protons and acts over long range → becomes dominant outside the nucleus.

Key Insight (Very Important)

✅ Inside nucleus → Strong force dominates → nucleons are bound
✅ Outside nucleus → Coulomb force dominates → repulsion creates barrier
✅ This barrier is the origin of alpha tunneling

Tunneling Probability (Gamow Factor)

The probability of alpha particle tunneling through the barrier is:

$$ P \propto e^{-2\gamma} $$

$$ \gamma = \frac{1}{\hbar} \int_{R}^{r_2} \sqrt{2m\,(V(r) - E)} \, dr $$

  • $R$: Nuclear radius
  • $r_2$: Turning point where $V(r)=E$
  • $V(r)$: Coulomb potential
  • $E = Q$: Alpha particle energy

Key insight: Tunneling probability depends exponentially on barrier → small change in $Q$ or $Z$ drastically changes half-life.
