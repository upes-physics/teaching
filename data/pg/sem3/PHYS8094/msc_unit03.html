Concept first (what students should understand)

Incoming particle (projectile): a charged particle/ion coming in with beam energy E (examples: proton, α, heavy ion).

Target nucleus: the nucleus it strikes (set by Z, A). Bigger Z increases Coulomb repulsion.

Impact parameter (b): the “miss distance” between the incoming trajectory and the target center. Large b → distant encounter; small b → strong overlap/contact.

Collision mechanisms (short but complete)

Elastic scattering (distant; Coulomb-dominated): projectile and target remain the same; mainly direction changes. Typically at large b.

Inelastic / Coulomb excitation (peripheral): no strong nuclear overlap, but the time-varying Coulomb field can excite the nucleus (rotations/vibrations). Same particles out, but with excitation energy.

Direct reactions (grazing; surface interaction): surface overlap—interaction with one/few nucleons (transfer, knockout, inelastic). Strong “memory” of entrance channel and structured angular distributions.

Compound nucleus / capture (close collision): strong overlap forms an excited composite; energy gets shared among many nucleons; decay looks statistical (evaporation/γ). Happens for small b and sufficient energy relative to the Coulomb barrier.

Deep-inelastic (heavy ions; partial overlap): dissipative collisions with large energy loss and nucleon exchange without full fusion (b in an intermediate overlap region for heavy ions).
