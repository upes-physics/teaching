Theoretical Concept

Concept: Fermi–Dirac and Bose–Einstein Statistics

Quantum particles obey different statistical distributions depending on their quantum nature. Fermions, which have half-integer spin, obey Fermi–Dirac statistics and are subject to the Pauli exclusion principle, limiting the occupation of each single-particle state to one particle. Bosons, with integer spin, obey Bose–Einstein statistics and can occupy the same state in arbitrarily large numbers. Their mean occupations are $\langle n_k \rangle = [e^{\beta(E_k - \mu)} \pm 1]^{-1}$, with $+1$ for fermions and $-1$ for bosons. Temperature and chemical potential determine how particles are distributed among energy levels, while at low temperature fermions approach a Fermi level and bosons can accumulate strongly in low-energy states.

Q3. Effect of Temperature
