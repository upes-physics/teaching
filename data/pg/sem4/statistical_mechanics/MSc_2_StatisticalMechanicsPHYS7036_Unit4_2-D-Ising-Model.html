Theoretical Concept

Concept: 2D Ising Model and Ferromagnetic Phase Transition

The 2D Ising model describes a lattice of interacting spins that can point either up or down, with neighbouring spins favouring parallel alignment when the exchange interaction \(J > 0\). The competition between this interaction and thermal fluctuations produces a phase transition at the critical temperature \(T_c \simeq 2.269 \, J/k_B\). Below \(T_c\), the system develops long-range magnetic order and a non-zero magnetization, whereas above \(T_c\), thermal fluctuations produce a disordered state with magnetization approaching zero. The Metropolis algorithm samples spin configurations according to the Boltzmann probability \(P(\Delta E) = \min[1, e^{-\Delta E / (k_B T)}]\), allowing the phase transition to be observed computationally.

2D Lattice
1. System Hamiltonian (Energy)

2. Metropolis Flip Probability
\(P(\Delta E) = \min(1, e^{-\Delta E / (k_B T)})\)

Q3. Why Does the System Become Disordered at High Temperature?

Set \(B = 0\) and compare the simulation at \(T = 1.0 \, J/k_B\) and \(T = 4.0 \, J/k_B\). Examine individual spin flips as the simulation runs. Why are energetically unfavourable spin flips more frequently accepted at the higher temperature?

Correct Answer: A The simulator uses the Metropolis rule \(P(\Delta E) = \min(1, e^{-\Delta E/(k_B T)})\). For \(\Delta E > 0\), increasing \(T\) makes the exponential suppression weaker, so energetically unfavourable flips become much more likely.

Correct Answer: A The Hamiltonian used by the simulator contains the field contribution \(H = -J \sum_{\langle i,j \rangle} s_i s_j - B \sum_i s_i\), so an external field energetically favours spins aligned with the field.
