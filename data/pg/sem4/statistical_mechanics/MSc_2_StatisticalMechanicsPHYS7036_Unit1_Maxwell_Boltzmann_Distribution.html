Concept: Maxwell–Boltzmann Speed Distribution

The Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution describes the range of molecular speeds in a classical ideal gas at thermal equilibrium. The distribution depends on both temperature ($T$) and molar mass ($M$): increasing temperature shifts the distribution toward higher speeds, while increasing molecular mass shifts it toward lower speeds. The distribution is characterized by three different speeds—the most probable speed ($v_{mp}$), average speed ($v_{avg}$), and RMS speed ($v_{rms}$)—which satisfy ($v_{mp} < v_{avg} < v_{rms}$). These speeds scale as ($\sqrt{T/M}$), providing a direct connection between microscopic molecular motion and macroscopic thermodynamic temperature.

5 Questions

Q1. Effect of Temperature

Set the molar mass to $M = 28\text{ g/mol}$. Run the simulator at $T = 300\text{ K}$ and then at $T = 1200\text{ K}$. What happens to the Maxwell–Boltzmann speed distribution when the temperature is increased by a factor of 4?

Q2. Effect of Molecular Mass

Set the temperature to $T = 300\text{ K}$. Compare $M = 4\text{ g/mol}$ and $M = 131\text{ g/mol}$ using the simulator. Which observation is correct?

Q3. Most Probable, Average and RMS Speeds

Choose any fixed temperature and molar mass and examine the three vertical markers displayed by the simulator. What ordering of the characteristic speeds do you observe from left to right along the speed axis?

Q4. Comparing Different Gases

Use the simulator's Helium and Nitrogen presets, corresponding to approximately $T = 298\text{ K}, M = 4\text{ g/mol}$ and $T = 300\text{ K}, M = 28\text{ g/mol}$, respectively. Which gas has the larger most probable speed?

Q5. Scaling of Molecular Speed with Temperature

Keep $M = 28\text{ g/mol}$ fixed and compare $T = 300\text{ K}$ with $T = 1200\text{ K}$. By approximately what factor does each of $v_{mp}$, $v_{avg}$, and $v_{rms}$ increase?

These five questions form a structured conceptual progression: temperature effect → mass effect → three characteristic speeds → comparison of gases → quantitative $\sqrt{T/M}$ scaling. The simulator directly provides the temperature/mass controls and the three speed markers needed for these observations.
