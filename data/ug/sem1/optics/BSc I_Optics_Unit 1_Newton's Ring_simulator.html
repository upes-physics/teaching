2. Background, Concept & Theory

Newton's Rings is a classic interference pattern formed by the division of amplitude when light reflects between a spherical lens surface and an adjacent flat glass plate. The enclosed air/liquid film has a variable thickness \(t\) that increases quadratically outwards from the point of contact.

When monochromatic light falls normally onto this setup, light reflects from both the bottom curved surface of the lens and the top flat surface of the glass plate. The ray reflected from the lower boundary (glass to air/medium) undergoes a phase change of \(\pi\) radians (path change of \(\lambda/2\)). Consequently, the central contact point (\(t=0\)) appears dark due to destructive interference.

3. Governing Formulas & Equations

Dark Ring Diameter (\(D_m\))

$$D_m^2 = \frac{4 m \lambda R}{n}$$
\(m = 0, 1, 2...\) order of dark ring

Bright Ring Diameter (\(D_m\))

$$D_m^2 = \frac{2(2m-1)\lambda R}{n}$$
\(m = 1, 2, 3...\) order of bright ring

Air Gap Thickness (\(t\))

$$t = \frac{r^2}{2R} = \frac{D_m^2}{8R}$$
Film thickness at radial distance \(r\)
