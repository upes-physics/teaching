2. Background, Concept & Theory

Laurent's Half-Shade Polarimeter is an optical instrument designed to measure the angle of optical rotation \(\theta\) produced by an optically active substance (such as aqueous sugar solutions) with high precision.

Due to the human eye's inability to accurately judge the exact angle of maximum extinction or maximum brightness of light, Laurent introduced a Half-Shade Plate consisting of two semicircular halves:

  • Glass Half: Made of optically inactive glass which absorbs light equally to the quartz half without introducing any phase retardation. Light emerging from it remains linearly polarized along angle \((\theta - \alpha)\).
  • Quartz Half: A half-wave plate (\(\lambda/2\)) cut parallel to its optic axis. It introduces a phase difference of \(\pi\) (\(180^\circ\)) between extraordinary and ordinary rays. If incident light enters at an angle \(\alpha\) relative to its optic axis, the emerging light's plane of polarization is symmetrically rotated to \((\theta + \alpha)\).

When the analyzer is positioned perpendicular to the bisector of the two polarization directions (\(\phi = \theta + 90^\circ\)), both halves transmit equal, minimal light intensity (\(I_0 \sin^2\alpha\)). This Equal Dim Match Point offers maximum visual contrast sensitivity to tiny angular changes in analyzer position.

3. Governing Formulas & Equations

Biot's Law of Optical Rotation

$$\theta = [\alpha]_\lambda^T \cdot L \cdot C$$
\([\alpha]\) in \(^\circ\cdot\text{dm}^{-1}\cdot(\text{g/mL})^{-1}\), \(L\) in dm, \(C\) in g/mL

Transmitted Intensities

$$I_{Glass} = I_0 \cos^2(\phi - \theta + \alpha)$$
$$I_{Quartz} = I_0 \cos^2(\phi - \theta - \alpha)$$
\(\phi\): Analyzer angle, \(\theta\): Solution rotation, \(\alpha\): Half-shade angle

Equal Intensity Match Condition

$$\phi_{match} = \theta + 90^\circ \quad \text{(Dim Match)}$$
$$\phi_{match} = \theta \quad \text{(Bright Match)}$$
Sensitivity is highest at the equal dim position
