Alpha Particle (α) Interactions

Heavy Charged Particle Physics (Bethe-Bloch Formula)

Alpha particles ($^4 ext{He}^{2+}$) are heavy, doubly charged particles. They interact primarily through inelastic Coulomb scattering with atomic electrons, producing intense ionization along a short, straight track.

Key Features:

    • Bragg Peak: As the particle slows down, its rate of energy loss ($dE/dx$) increases, reaching a maximum near the end of its range before dropping rapidly to zero. • Minimal Deflection: Due to their large mass (~7300x electron mass), alphas suffer negligible elastic scattering and travel in nearly straight paths. • High Linear Energy Transfer (LET): High ionization density leads to severe localized damage in biological tissue. Short range (a few cm in air, micrometers in tissue).

Beta Particle (β⁻ / β⁺) Interactions

Light Charged Particle Physics

Beta particles are energetic electrons or positrons emitted during nuclear decay. Because of their light mass (1/1836 u), their interaction profile differs significantly from heavy ions:

    • Multiple Coulomb Scattering: Beta particles undergo significant elastic scattering with target nuclei, resulting in erratic, tortuous trajectories. • Collisional vs Radiative Loss: Energy loss occurs via ionization (collisional) and Bremsstrahlung (radiative X-ray emission when accelerating near heavy Z nuclei). • Bremsstrahlung Yield: Scaled by $E \cdot Z / 800$. High-Z absorbers like Lead emit high secondary X-ray radiation. Therefore, Low-Z materials (e.g. Acrylic/Lucite, Plastic) are preferred for Beta shielding.

Gamma & X-Ray Photon Interactions

Exponential Attenuation: $I(x) = I_0 \, e^{-\mu x}$

Photons are uncharged and do not experience continuous deceleration like charged particles. Instead, they are removed from the primary beam via single probabilistic events. The three primary mechanisms are:

    1. Photoelectric Effect: Photon is completely absorbed by a bound electron. Dominates at low energies ($E < 0.1$ MeV) and high Z ($\sigma \propto Z^4 / E^3$). 2. Compton Scattering: Photon scatters elastically off a loosely bound electron, losing part of its energy. Dominates at intermediate energies (0.5 - 5 MeV). 3. Pair Production: Photon transforms into an electron-positron pair near a nucleus. Occurs only above threshold $E > 1.022$ MeV ($\sigma \propto Z^2$).

Neutron Interactions with Matter

Nuclear Interactions (Uncharged Heavy Particles)

Neutrons have no electric charge and interact exclusively through nuclear forces with target atomic nuclei, rather than atomic electrons.

    • Elastic Scattering (n, n): Neutron bounces off nucleus, transferring kinetic energy. Energy transfer is maximized when target mass matches neutron mass ($A pprox 1$, e.g., Hydrogen in Water/Polyethylene). • Inelastic Scattering (n, n'γ): Energetic neutron excites the nucleus, emitting gamma photons upon target de-excitation. Heavy nuclei (Lead) slow fast neutrons efficiently via inelastic collisions. • Neutron Capture (n, γ): Thermalized (slow) neutrons are absorbed by nuclei (e.g. Boron-10, Cadmium, Hydrogen), releasing capture gammas.

Effective Neutron Shielding Strategy

Step 1: Moderation

Slow down fast neutrons using light-element moderators high in Hydrogen (Water, Paraffin Wax, Polyethylene).

Step 2: Capture

Absorb thermal neutrons using elements with large thermal neutron capture cross-sections (Boron-10, Cadmium).

Step 3: Secondary Gamma Shielding

Attenuate secondary capture gammas using high-Z materials (Lead or Heavy Concrete outer layer).
