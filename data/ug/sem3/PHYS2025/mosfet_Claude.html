01 · Foundations

What is a MOSFET?

Metal–Oxide–Semiconductor Field-Effect Transistor. Like the JFET it's voltage-controlled and unipolar — but the gate is a metal (or polysilicon) plate sitting on a thin insulating oxide, completely isolated from the channel.

Structure

Two heavily doped regions — Source and Drain — are diffused into a lightly doped substrate (body) of the opposite type.

  • A thin layer of SiO₂ (silicon dioxide) insulates the body from a metal gate deposited on top.
  • Because the gate is separated from the semiconductor by an insulator, essentially no DC current ever flows into the gate — input resistance can exceed 10¹⁰ Ω.
  • The body is usually strapped internally to the source, leaving three usable terminals: G, D, S.

Two families

The gate's electric field can either remove carriers from an existing channel or induce a brand new one — giving two distinct devices:

  • Depletion-type (D-MOSFET): a channel is physically built in during manufacture. It conducts at VGS = 0 and can be depleted (narrowed) or enhanced (widened) by the gate voltage.
  • Enhancement-type (E-MOSFET): no channel exists at all at VGS = 0. One must be induced by pushing VGS past a threshold — by far the more common type in digital ICs.

The square law

Both types share the same current equation once you know the effective "overdrive" voltage VOV = VGS − Vth:

  • Below threshold: cut-off, ID ≈ 0.
  • Low VDS: triode / ohmic region, acts as a voltage-controlled resistor.
  • High VDS: saturation, ID = K(VGS − Vth)², roughly constant with VDS.

02 · Regions of operation

Cut-off, triode, saturation

The same three regions as a JFET, but the cut-off boundary is defined by a threshold Vth that can sit on either side of zero, depending on the type.

Triode / ohmic

VDS < VOV. The induced or built-in channel spans source to drain along its full length; the device behaves like a voltage-controlled resistor.

Saturation / active

VDS ≥ VOV. The channel pinches off at the drain end; ID = K·VOV² and stays nearly flat as VDS rises further — the region used for amplification.

Cut-off

VGS ≤ Vth. For an enhancement device this means VGS below threshold — no channel yet. For a depletion device it means VGS negative enough to fully pinch the built-in channel.
