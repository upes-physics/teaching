    Small-signal foundations

    What does "common" mean?

    The common terminal is shared by the input and output circuits. It is usually treated as an AC reference, even when it sits at a nonzero DC bias voltage.

    Why does transconductance matter?

    Transconductance gm tells how strongly a small base-emitter voltage change controls collector current. Near room temperature, gm is approximately IC divided by 25 mV.

    Why can the output clip?

    The quiescent point must leave room for the output to move in both directions. Too much signal, current or resistance pushes the transistor toward cutoff or saturation.

    What is missing from this model?

    Coupling capacitors, transistor output resistance, junction capacitances, exact bias networks and frequency roll-off are omitted to keep the learning model clear.

    Guided exploration

    Try three revealing experiments

    Each button loads a useful setup. Read the observation prompt, then keep moving the sliders.

    Experiment 1

    Make a CE stage clip

    Increase input amplitude and collector resistance. The ideal gain rises, but the DC operating point leaves too little clean output swing.

    Experiment 2

    Challenge the CB input

    Use a large source resistance with the common-base input. Its very low input impedance absorbs most of the source signal.

    Experiment 3

    Use CC as a buffer

    Drive the emitter follower from a high-resistance source. Its high input impedance preserves the signal while its output impedance stays low.
